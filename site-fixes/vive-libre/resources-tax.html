Resources · Tax & money

Financial planning before you move.

What to arrange with home-country advisers first, and the costs to budget for when you buy.

Buying costs

On top of the price, budget roughly 11% to 14% for taxes and fees. Resale homes carry transfer tax (ITP); new builds carry VAT plus stamp duty. Notary, land registry and legal fees make up the rest. See the full breakdown in the buying process.

Becoming tax resident

Spending more than 183 days a year in Spain, or having your main base of interests here, generally makes you tax resident. Residents are taxed on worldwide income, and there are annual declarations for assets held abroad.

Ongoing property taxes

  • IBI: the annual local property tax
  • Non-resident income tax: payable by non-resident owners even when the home isn't let
  • Wealth tax: can apply to larger estates, under regional rules

Mortgages in Spain

Spanish banks lend to non-residents as well as residents, though usually at a lower loan-to-value, and the terms often differ between the two. Getting an agreement in principle before you view gives you a clear budget and puts you in a stronger position to negotiate.

  • Deposit: non-residents typically need a larger deposit than residents, so plan for more of the price in cash
  • Fixed, variable or mixed rates: variable rates track the Euribor, so weigh certainty against cost
  • Paperwork: expect to show your NIE, proof of income, tax returns, bank statements and existing debts
  • Extra costs: valuation, arrangement fees and any life or home insurance the bank asks for
  • Currency: if you earn in pounds or another currency, a euro mortgage carries exchange-rate risk

A specialist mortgage broker can compare Spanish lenders and lenders in your home country, and some arrange finance against assets you already own. We'll introduce you to brokers who work with international buyers.

Talk to advisers early

The biggest savings come from planning before you move: when you arrive, how you hold the property, pensions, inheritance and currency transfers. Speak to a cross-border tax adviser who understands both countries.

This is a general overview, not tax, mortgage or financial advice. Rates and allowances change and differ between regions. We'll connect you with independent tax specialists who work with international buyers every day.
