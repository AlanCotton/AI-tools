The buying process

Buying a home on the Costa Blanca, step by step.

Buying in Spain follows a clear sequence, but it isn't the one you know from home. Here's every stage, how long it usually takes, what it costs and who does what, with us beside you throughout.

The steps

Eight steps from first plans to your own front door.

For a resale home, offer to keys usually takes two to three months. The groundwork before you view can start whenever you're ready.

01

Get ready to buy

Before you view · allow 4 to 8 weeks

A little preparation means you can move quickly when the right home appears.

  • NIE number: your foreigner's identification number, needed to sign anything. Apply at a Spanish consulate or by appointment in Spain.
  • Spanish bank account: for the deposit, the purchase and your direct debits.
  • Finance: if you need a mortgage, get an agreement in principle so you know your budget.
  • Your brief: we agree budget, must-haves and the feel of the place you're after.
02

Search and viewings

Weeks to months

We search the whole market, every agency plus off-market homes, and bring you a shortlist worth your time. Viewing trips are planned around you, and you get our honest read on each property: aspect, winter sun, noise, access and what's planned next door.

03

Offer and negotiation

A few days

When you find the one, we negotiate on your behalf. Price is only part of it: we also agree what's included, such as furniture and fittings, and a completion date that suits you.

04

Reservation

Within a week of agreeing the price

A small reservation deposit takes the home off the market while your lawyer runs the checks. Pay it into your lawyer's client account where you can, never in cash, and make sure the agreement says when it's refundable.

05

Legal checks and survey

1 to 3 weeks

Your own independent lawyer, never the seller's, checks the property and the seller before you commit further.

  • The nota simple from the land registry: who owns it, and any mortgages or charges
  • The registry and the catastro match on size, boundaries and buildings
  • Pools, extensions and conversions are legal and registered
  • No unpaid IBI, community fees or utility bills, which can follow the property
  • For newer homes, the licence of first occupation or its equivalent

This is also the time for an independent survey, especially on an older villa.

06

Private purchase contract

Once the checks are clear

You and the seller sign a private contract, usually an arras contract, and you pay a deposit, commonly 10% of the price less the reservation deposit. It fixes the price, what's included and the completion date.

Under the usual form of arras, if you pull out you lose the deposit; if the seller pulls out, they return it twice over. Your lawyer will confirm exactly which terms apply.

07

Completion at the notary

Usually 6 to 10 weeks after the contract

You and the seller sign the title deed, the escritura, in front of a notary. You pay the balance, usually by bank draft or transfer, and receive the keys the same day.

Can't be there? You can give your lawyer a power of attorney to sign for you. If the seller isn't resident in Spain, 3% of the price is held back and paid to the tax office on their behalf; your lawyer handles this.

08

After the keys

The first weeks
  • Your lawyer pays the purchase taxes, due within a month of signing
  • The deed is registered in your name at the land registry
  • Electricity, water, internet and insurance move into your name
  • IBI and community fees are set up by direct debit

Then the good part begins, and we're still on hand: builders, interiors, schools, residency and introductions to new friends.

Buying costs

Budget around 11% to 14% on top of the price.

Who's involved

The people around your purchase.

Us

Your buyer's agent

We work only for you: we find the home, negotiate the price and keep every step on track.

Lawyer

Your independent lawyer

Runs the legal checks, writes or reviews the contracts and pays your taxes. Always your own, never the seller's.

Notary

The notary

A public official who witnesses the deed and confirms identities. The notary is neutral and doesn't advise either side.

Bank

Bank or broker

Your Spanish account and, if you need one, the mortgage. A broker can compare lenders for you.

Surveyor

The surveyor

Not routine in Spain, but worth it: damp, structure and costly repairs found before you commit.

Gestor

The gestor

Handles the paperwork afterwards: utilities, tax registrations and the many small forms Spain loves.

This page is general guidance, not legal or tax advice. Your own lawyer confirms every step for your purchase. For the checks we insist on and how to avoid scams, see Buying safely.
